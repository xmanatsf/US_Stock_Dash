Fab5 Cross-Source Synthesis · Run 6 5 September 2026 · addendum 6 September 51 reports · seven houses · 11 Aug – 6 Sep

Own what funds itself.

The binding constraint moved again, and every house moved with it independently. Chip supply bound 2023–24. Power and cooling bound 2025 to mid-2026. From the second half of 2026 the binding constraint is credit. Fundamentals did not weaken — they strengthened almost everywhere this run looked. What changed is that the marginal dollar funding the buildout is now borrowed, increasingly by counterparties who are not hyperscalers, and the market has begun to price the difference.

The four-layer read

Where the argument actually sits
Layer 1

Operational fundamentals

Stronger than Run 5, with the constraint moved downstream of the fab. Cloud backlog approaching $1.7trn on ISI's count against forward cloud capex just over $620bn — roughly 4x cover, with sales revisions exceeding capex revisions. AWS +37% at a record 39.4% operating margin; Google Cloud +82% with margin 20.7% → 35.6%.

What binds is conversion, not demand. Amazon's raise from $200bn to $220bn was entirely memory inflation with zero incremental capacity. DELL says upside is limited by memory/SSD supply and site readiness, not demand.

The 2027 question has been rewritten from “can you get the order” to “can you light the equipment up.”

Layer 2

Market structure

Genuinely contradictory, and both halves are real. Bullish: mega-cap tech under-owned by 129bps versus the S&P and widening; ETF shorts covering a sixth straight week; the S&P ex-AI up nearly 8% since late June while the index ranged — the AI complex has been a net drag. SOX at 19.3x NTM, below the S&P's 19.5x.

Bearish: CFTC non-dealer Nasdaq positioning turned negative for the first time since May 2025 on a record −$21bn week, during a +7% rally. Ten stocks are 38% of the S&P against a 27% dot-com peak, with three-month implied correlation at generational lows.

Extreme concentration plus low correlation is mathematically fragile. The rotation Run 5 called for has been happening inside the complex, not out of it.

Layer 3

Financing and credit

This is where the run's information is, and five houses reached it independently in ten days without cross-citation. Red-Ring priced it first on 24 August (AVGO and NVDA CDS at all-time wides of 126.2bp and 86.7bp); ISI framed it four days later, swapping its fourth bear trigger from equities to IG Tech credit spreads.

The cleanest signal is the tenant split: AMZN-tenant data-centre bonds at 5.9–6.5% YTW against CoreWeave/NVDA-tenant paper at 8–9%. The credit market is discriminating by tenant quality, publicly.

Price signal preceded framework by four days. Worth watching for again.

Layer 4

Macro and policy

An unresolvable September and a 2027 collision. On 3 September, reading the same Waller speech: GS put hike odds at ~60%, ISI at “under 50 per cent”, and TMTB read it as a priced cut with ~60% odds of action within weeks. The same number, opposite directions.

ISI's three-wave inflation model now names AI capex at ~2pp of the ~53% July diffusion index, with the channel identified explicitly as memory-chip costs.

The first time in this project's history that the AI capex cycle appears as a quantified input to the Fed's reaction function rather than as a sector story.

Where all seven houses agree

Six points · no source dissents
1

2027 capex goes up, not down

Every house that models it raised. No source cut a 2027 number this run.

2

Memory pricing is at or near record, and near-term prints are still rising

DRAM gross margin 87%, NAND 80%; DRAM ASPs +5x and NAND +4x since 1Q25; 18 consecutive weeks of spot increases; export prices +135% y/y in 1H26.

3

Power is the physical binding constraint and holds the margin pool

Two unrelated ISI desks reached this independently in the same week without citing each other. NSR: “grid capacity, not chip supply, is emerging as the scarcest resource.”

4

The Marvell $120bn headline is a ceiling, not a base case

JPM, NSR and MS reached this independently: warrant-implied commercial stickiness, not RPO, and explicitly not a core TPU accelerator order. NSR's own 2028 estimate is ~$10bn. Only the market read it as displacement.

5

Custom silicon versus GPU is not zero-sum

MS's CoWoS allocation build has NVIDIA at 780k wafers (2026e) → 1,222k (2027e), Broadcom 300k → 484k and AMD 60k → 530k — all three scaling simultaneously. The constraint is packaging capacity, not competitive share.

6

Credit is the new binding constraint

Reached in sequence by Red-Ring (price), ISI (framework), TMTB (structure) and JPM (valuation instruction) — without cross-citation.

The memory cycle top

Unresolved · top item

For a much later top: UBS dates DRAM to 2Q28 and NAND to 4Q27 and reframes the constraint as affordability; MS shows persistent total-DRAM deficits of −4% to −17% through the horizon; NSR shows margins locked at floors above prior-cycle peaks. NSR itself supplies the caveat the bears need: supply agreements are temporary protection whose favourable terms would not be renewed in a real downturn; only HBM's buyer concentration is structural. And you cannot simultaneously assume locked volume is low risk and full participation in spot upside.

The recommendation

One stance, three instructions

Own the AI that funds itself; underweight the AI that has to borrow — and express the view through volatility, not direction.

Five houses converged on credit as the binding variable in ten days without citing each other. Red-Ring priced it first on 24 August (AVGO and NVDA CDS at all-time wides of 126.2bp and 86.7bp; the tenant-yield split). ISI framed it four days later, swapping its fourth bear trigger from equities to IG Tech spreads — "the second most important chart there is." TMTB structured it: Broadcom guaranteeing part of the senior tranche of the ~$100bn raise that funds its own largest customer. JPM turned it into a valuation instruction. Morgan Stanley supplied the counterparty arithmetic. When five houses converge on a variable in ten days without cross-citation, that variable is the one to trade.

The expression is volatility because one Waller speech produced four readings across three houses, spanning both directions — GS at ~60% odds of a September hike (~65% in its own quick hits), ISI under 50%, TMTB at ~60% odds of action on a 35bp+ cut. That is not resolvable by analysis before the event. Layered on it: the dated correction window, a CTA book with 4.5x downside skew, and VIX at the fifth percentile.

Instruction 1

Long the self-funders against the balance-sheet-dependent tail. Emanuel's Low Leverage Leaders basket is already the right shape — NVDA, MU, AMD, PLTR, LRCX, ANET, MRVL, ALAB — ex-SpaceX, given that all three of its large AI compute contracts are cancelable on 90 days' notice.

Instruction 2

Buy the September–January optionality rather than a direction. XLK one-month implied 23.6% against 27.7% realized; VIX 14.43. Long duration is the corroborating hedge, not a separate view.

Instruction 3

Hold the memory trim at the un-contracted tier only, and size it small. Three houses are against it; one balanced source is half for it.

What is not being recommended

This is not a reduction of AI exposure. Backlog cover is ~4x with sales revisions exceeding capex revisions; mega-cap tech is under-owned by 129bps and widening; SOX trades at 19.3x NTM, below the S&P's 19.5x; and the S&P ex-AI is up nearly 8% since late June — the AI complex has been a net drag, not an overheating source. Cutting gross exposure here would be trading the narrative rather than the evidence.

Medium-term outlook

Quarters
Base case

The buildout continues at a scale nobody in this project has yet had to mark down, and returns accrue where they have been accruing — to the power layer, to upstream enablers, to vendors who can self-finance, and to memory makers whose contracts are locked. Compute capacity roughly quadruples to ~120GW by 2028 on MS's numbers.

The 2027 constraint is energisation, not orders. The buyer base keeps broadening — which reduces dependence on a few hyperscalers while raising the dispersion of customer credit quality.

Bull case

The AI-disclosure multiple generalises. A quantified AI contribution earns roughly three-plus turns of EV/sales and near-zero without one — Snowflake 9.3x → 12.4x and held; Datadog 10.9x → 13.9x → 16.9x; MongoDB zero turns on a solid AI-silent quarter.

If more of the complex can produce per-SKU quantified disclosure, the multiple compression of the last six weeks reverses with no change in fundamentals.

Bear case

ISI's ADI analog: once NTM EPS clears the prior cycle peak by a low-double-digit percentage, the multiple historically absorbs the beats — 38% and 60% compression after 15% and 11% overshoots. This cycle's overshoot is 30% with only 29% compression so far.

If the analog holds, the correction is a multiple event with earnings still rising — the hardest kind to trade and the easiest to argue away in real time.

The bear case

What breaks the thesis

The thesis does not break on a demand miss. It breaks if financing reverses before fundamentals stall.

Red-Ring identifies the mechanism: the platform migrates GPU residual value, utilisation, customer credit and re-lease risk into private credit, insurance money and project finance — creating the possibility that multiple financing vehicles mark down residual value and tighten leverage simultaneously, forcing project cancellations into an order book that is still nominally strong. Morgan Stanley identifies the counterparty: CoreWeave tenant debt to $116.2bn by 2028, with tenant-level leverage compounding faster than the assets are being de-risked. TMTB identifies the correlated structure: Broadcom guaranteeing part of the senior tranche of the debt that funds its own largest customer, so an Anthropic disappointment produces falling orders and guarantee payouts at once.

The observable precursor is not a bankruptcy — it is spread widening in IG Tech while equities are still making highs. That specific divergence is what the current price structure would produce first, and it is why the monitoring order is credit before equities.

The slower break

Cost-push ASP inflation. Vera Rubin and Grace Blackwell system prices are reportedly up ~15–17% attributed to DRAM cost, and NVIDIA's own gross margin troughs at 71–72% on memory it cannot fully offset. Broadcom's MW gross margin is modelled falling 77.3% → 67.4% by FY28e. If ASP inflation is cost-push rather than value-add, gross margin and customer ROI become the next constraint. Volume growth is no longer automatically converting into margin expansion anywhere in the hardware stack.

The multiple version

Lipacis's ADI analog. Once NTM EPS clears the prior cycle peak by a low-double-digit percentage, the multiple has historically done the work of absorbing the beats — 38% and 60% compression after 15% and 11% overshoots. This cycle's overshoot is 30% with only 29% compression so far. The framework implies AVGO at $283–$174 against Lipacis's own published bear case of $341, and the note does not acknowledge the conflict. If the analog holds, the correction is a multiple event with earnings still rising — the hardest kind to trade and the easiest to argue away in real time.

And what would kill the recommendation

IG Tech spreads tightening decisively through 100bp while the tenant-quality spread compresses. That would say the credit market has looked at the vendor-financing structures and decided they are additive capital rather than relocated leverage — NSR's case. If it happens, drop the short leg and stay long the complex outright.
